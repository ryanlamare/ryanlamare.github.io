01 — COLOUR

The palette

One warm key. Paper cream and ink carry everything; vermilion is the single accent; the rest are figure colours, sampled from authentic Isotype sources.

Reach for vermilion only to mean “look here.” The warm and cool families (terracotta through ochre) are category colours — use them in order so multi-part figures stay coherent. Maroon is deep emphasis; grey is a quiet neutral.

Colour rule: monochrome by default, colour only for meaning.

A mark is ink with a single vermilion accent — your signature device, the red picking out the telling detail (the second data column, one factory window, the pivot of the scales). Recolour a whole mark only inside a figure, where colour encodes a category — the Isotype principle: same form, new colour, new meaning. The one deliberate exception is the trade-unions fist — solid vermilion, the emblem of collective power.

05 — DO & DON'T

House rules

DO

  • Let one figure replace a list of bullets.
  • Keep vermilion rare and meaningful.
  • Use category colours in order for multi-part figures.
  • Hold the grid; leave generous space.
  • Recolour marks only to encode meaning.

DON'T

  • No gradients, glows, or glassmorphism.
  • No line icons or rainbow pictograms.
  • No accent stripes beneath titles.
  • No bright primary yellow/blue — use the muted Isotype versions.
  • No flashy transitions.
