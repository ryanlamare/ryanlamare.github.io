SESSION 1 · PRINCIPLES

Characteristics of negotiations

1
There are two or more parties that need each other (interdependence)
2
Something needs to be resolved: a sale, a conflict, a problem, an issue or a decision
3
There is no fixed solution
4
They involve give and take, and mutual adjustment
5
They involve the management of “intangibles”
6
They involve “dilemmas of honesty” and “dilemmas of trust”

How George Lucas kept the Star Wars rights

  • Fox let George Lucas give up an extra $500,000 directing fee in return for keeping the licensing and merchandising rights
  • He kept the rights to the sequels too. At the time, successful merchandising seemed limited to animated characters
  • Each side gave up something it valued less for something it valued more

SESSION 1 · IN THE NEWS

Opera houses have alternatives too

  • In 1989, days before its season opened, the Lyric Opera of Chicago fired Luciano Pavarotti. He had cancelled 26 of the 41 performances he was booked for there
  • In 2006, Roberto Alagna walked off La Scala’s stage in the middle of Aida. His understudy went on in his jeans
  • A star’s leverage depends on the house’s alternative

SESSION 1 · DEBRIEF

The Strategic Negotiations framework

1
Context
Hopes, fears, power and history
2
Structure
Is this a one-time negotiation or part of an ongoing relationship? Are people representing themselves, or are agents involved?
3
Process
Did your process realise the integrative potential of the issues? Was it fair to both parties? Were biases present?
4
Strategy
Distributive or integrative? Is it better to not reach a settlement at all (BATNA)?
5
Outcomes
Have both sides’ interests been addressed? Can the agreement be implemented? Have relationships been preserved?
Both of you negotiated for someone else. Whose interests were you serving?

SESSION 1 · YOUR NEGOTIATIONS

The framework in *your* world

Pick one real negotiation coming up at work, then answer five questions:
1
Context: what are the hopes, fears, power and history on each side?
2
Structure: is it a one-time negotiation or part of an ongoing relationship? Are you representing yourself, or someone else?
3
Process: how will you find the integrative potential in the issues, and keep the process fair to both sides?
4
Strategy: distributive or integrative? And what is your BATNA if you don’t reach a settlement?
5
Outcomes: what agreement would address both sides’ interests, and could you implement it?
AT YOUR TABLES: Brainstorm together, pick the negotiation with the most at stake, and choose one person to keep notes and share.
SESSION 1 · TAKEAWAYS

a few key takeaways...

What goes wrong?
Most often, we don’t even try to negotiate, or we prepare the wrong way
Know your BATNA
Every negotiation has an alternative, so know yours and think about theirs
Two main types
Distributive negotiation claims value, and integrative negotiation creates it
Why such different deals?
The same singer played the same role, and still the agreements were far apart
A framework
Context, structure, process, strategy and outcomes
Positions and interests
Opposing positions are often rooted in both conflicting and also shared interests
Effective negotiation is not only a list of things to do, it’s also a way of thinking
