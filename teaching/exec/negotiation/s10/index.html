SESSION 10 · IN THE NEWS

When “no” is the best answer

  • In 2015, Boston was the United States’ choice to bid for the 2024 Olympic Games
  • The US Olympic Committee wanted the mayor to sign a guarantee that would leave the city liable for any overruns. He refused: “I refuse to mortgage the future of the city away”
  • The committee dropped Boston the same day. He knew his BATNA, and he walked away

SESSION 10 · YOUR NEGOTIATIONS

Plan *your* next negotiation

Pick one real negotiation coming up at work, then answer four questions:
1
What is your BATNA, and what do you think is theirs?
2
What are their interests? What do they want, and why do they want it?
3
Which issues could you trade, and what else could you put on the table?
4
Who moves first, and how will the other side respond to your move?
AT YOUR TABLES: Brainstorm together, pick the negotiation with the most at stake, and choose one person to keep notes and share.
SESSION 10 · FINAL THOUGHTS

Some final thoughts

  • You’ve changed (you’ve learned how to create value)… but out there, they haven’t: be prepared!
  • Here, interests are well defined, but they are not always. Negotiation is an education about interests and solutions
  • It pays to help others win, and it doesn’t always have to cost you (much)! But you also need to be aware of situations where you’re incentivized to gain at the expense of others in the short term
  • Here, you’re supposed to “negotiate”. Out there, you have to “make it happen”, or see and create opportunities
SESSION 10 · FINAL THOUGHTS

Some final thoughts

  • Here, you’re supposed to agree. But in the real world, sometimes “no” is the best answer… know your BATNA!
  • Negotiation is an art and a science
  • You’ve gained a better understanding of the factors that underlie successful negotiation, but negotiation situations are complex and dynamic
  • Keep practicing the skills you’ve learned, and reflecting on your outcomes!
