SESSION 3 · WHY NEGOTIATE

The cost of not negotiating

The package you obtain has long-term consequences

Suppose at age 30, two equally qualified graduates get job offers for $50,000 a year. One negotiates and gets $55,000; the other accepts the $50,000 offer.

If they both receive 5% annual salary increases, at age 65 how long would the non-negotiator have to work to make up the difference?

Eight years longer!By 65 the gap has grown to about $450,000, around eight years of the $55,000 starting salary

Three types of issues

DistributiveDifferent preferences, same values: a typical “fixed pie”
IntegrativeDifferent preferences and different values: give on an issue you care less about to gain on one you care more about
CongruentSame preferences and same values: identify these! Ask questions, check assumptions

SESSION 3 · ON SCREEN

Michael Scott negotiates a buyout

  • Michael’s new paper company has taken Dunder Mifflin’s biggest clients, so Dunder Mifflin wants to buy it out. What it doesn’t know is that Michael’s company is broke
  • Dunder Mifflin offered $12,000, then $60,000. Michael turned both down and asked instead for his old job back, and for Pam and Ryan to be hired as salespeople
  • Michael’s side cared more about the jobs than the cash, and Dunder Mifflin got its clients back

SESSION 3 · CREATING VALUE

The ABCs of value creation

A
Acquire information
  • Ask what they want, and why they want it
  • Ask which issues matter most to them
  • Check your assumptions
B
Find the biggest pool of resourcesCreating value
  • Look for common ground
  • Trade what you care less about for what you care more about
  • Negotiate the whole package, not one issue at a time
C
Claim your shareClaiming value
  • Justify your position with objective criteria
  • Frame your concessions as gains
  • Know your BATNA
The goal of negotiation is to satisfy your own interests

SESSION 3 · CREATING VALUE

Contingent agreements

When the two sides disagree about the future, they can make the outcome depend on what actually happens
The deal

Jack Nicholson took a lower fee to play the Joker in Batman (1989), about $6 million, in return for a share of the box office and of the Joker merchandise

What happened

The film was a hit, and the deal reportedly paid him $50 million or more

Each side takes the bet it believes in, so both can leave better off

SESSION 3 · TAKEAWAYS

a few key takeaways...

Three types of issues
Distributive, integrative and congruent, and each calls for a different move
Where does value come from?
Trading what you care less about for what you care more about
Why do pairs miss shared interests?
Assumptions get in the way, so ask questions and check them
Why not split the difference?
Splitting every issue down the middle leaves money on the table
What if you disagree about the future?
Make the deal depend on what actually happens
It pays to help others win, and it doesn’t always have to cost you much!
