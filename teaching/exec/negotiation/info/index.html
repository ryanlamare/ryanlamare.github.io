Manipulating information

You know something the other side does not
Conceal information, or reveal misleading info
Reveal selected information truthfully
You want to find out what the other side knows

SESSION 8

Signaling, signal jamming, and screening

Three devices can be used to manipulate information:
Signaling
Favorable leakage of information, or actions taken to have the other side believe your information is good
Signal jamming
Acting to reduce or eliminate the leakage of unfavorable information
Screening
Forcing the other side to reveal that side’s true information

SESSION 8

Credible signals

The warranty
The seller must believe the car is of sufficient quality to not lose money by offering it
The expensive launch
Is there value in an expensive, useless, and uninformative product launch? You bet!
The marble countertop
Do we want our banks to have marble countertops and Nespresso machines? Of course!
A signal works only if it is too costly for the wrong type to fake
SESSION 8

The dog that didn’t bark

Signaling can get messy. Sometimes one side will expect a signal, and the other will not provide it
“The curious incident of the dog in the night-time”: signaling by not signaling
When Netflix said it would stop reporting its subscriber numbers, its shares fell 9% the next day: investors read the silence as slowing growth
“I’m not political. I don’t vote.”  “You are political, because you don’t vote.”
Keep this in mind when you’re hiring job candidates, too!
SESSION 8

Pooling and separating equilibria

Cleanliness is a potentially useful signal of the quality of a house. But all sellers know they need to clean their houses, so even those with terrible underlying problems are cleanCleaning away the problems is signal jamming, so cleanliness stops telling buyers anything
Pooling
All different types, good and bad, end up taking the same action, rendering it useless
Separating
The good types signal their goodness to the market by differentiating from the bad
How might you find a separating equilibrium when you're in the market for a house?
