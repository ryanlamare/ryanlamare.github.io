SESSION 9 · NEGOTIATING AS A TEAM

When six actors negotiated as one

  • In the first season of Friends, the six leads had different contracts, starting at $22,500 an episode
  • After the second season they negotiated together, for the same pay. The studio could have kept only four of them, and the six stood by each other
  • By 2002 each was paid $1 million an episode. A negotiating team that holds together has more power, but first it has to agree within itself
The cast of Friends would not be torn apart by contracts Off Camera
SESSION 9 · COALITIONS

How a coalition of eleven starts to break

  • In 12 Angry Men, eleven jurors are for a guilty verdict. One, alone, asks for a secret ballot, and abstains
  • If all eleven still vote guilty, he will go along with them. If anyone votes not guilty, they stay and talk it out
  • One vote turns. Coalitions form and break as a negotiation goes on, and it can take only one member to open one up

Exercise: Harborco

  • Read your role first (30–45 minutes): the letter to your role, then the general information, then your confidential role

  • You’re welcome to bring whatever you want into the negotiation, but please do not try to look for “solutions” to the game!

SESSION 9 · FAILURE IS YOUR FRIEND

Before your group decides, imagine that it failed

  • Freakonomics Radio’s “Failure Is Your Friend” looks at how groups fail, from the Challenger launch decision on, and at one way to catch it: the premortem
  • Just before a team commits, imagine it is a year later and the decision was a disaster. Each person gives a brief history of that disaster
  • It legitimises dissent, and rewards people for finding the flaws in a plan
Daniel Kahneman on the premortem Kepner-Tregoe
