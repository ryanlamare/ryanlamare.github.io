GAME THEORY

The two-day course I built for RRPF. Put a module on the laptop or the TV, and scan its QR codes with your phones to play the games.

I built all of this in collaboration with Claude Code, Anthropic’s AI coding tool. The teaching and nearly all the words come from my own course slides. I vibe coded the games and drawings by giving prompts to Claude, and iterated on every module slide by slide to get it exactly how I wanted it.

+The design

The look

IsotypePictorial statistics after Otto Neurath and Gerd Arntz. Flat pictograms, repeated, doing the explaining.
Mid-century modernThe drawn scenes, like the 1970 car lot, the jet and the airfield: flat colour, clean shapes and warm light.
BrutalismLeague Spartan at full weight on the covers, with giant wordmarks and numerals on a dark ground.
Constructivist restraintSharp geometry, generous space, no decoration for its own sake, and black-and-white contrast after the Factory Records mark.

The palette

One warm key. Paper cream and ink carry everything; vermilion is the single accent; the rest are figure colours, sampled from authentic Isotype sources. Reach for vermilion only to mean “look here.”

The teaching

  • Game theory is the art and science of strategic thinking, where your best course of action requires consideration of what the other side intends to do.
  • Every module is built around games the room plays on its own phones. The game comes first, and the theory is named afterwards.
  • A diagram beats a bullet list, so the picture carries the idea and the words stay short.
  • It is a class for executives, so the discussion is about them and not the slides.
