What was each side really buying?

The same aircraft, but different parts of it
Without it, the best deal was to share the summer: a couple of months each, half of what either of you needed. Without any deal, you bid against each other and the seller took the difference.
With it, both of you get the whole summer: one of you buys the Meridian at the guide, Harrow takes the cabin for its stage, and Larkfield flies what is left. Then you split the cost, and the sale afterwards.
Built on Robert J. House’s Ugli Orange case, and the orange in Fisher and Ury’s Getting to Yes
