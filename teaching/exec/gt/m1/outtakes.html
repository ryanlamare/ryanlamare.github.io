Slide 3 of 9: Granito Air

The cut slide, verbatim. A drawn top view of a Granito Air twin-engine jet with a GA monogram on a blue tail fin, above a row of five small spare engines on stands, labeled five engines in storage. Bullets revealed one at a time: Granito Air flies seven A330ceos out of Parma, and they are retiring them; five Trent 700 engines are sitting in storage. Those engines are worth millions, whole or in parts, and the airline wants the best deal possible. Several traders are competing for them, and you are one of them. The airline knows things about its own engines that you don't. Get this deal, and there are more engines behind it, and maybe the whole fleet. A closing red chip asks: how can common knowledge help us in this game?

Slide 6 of 9: Common knowledge and equilibrium, with the old matrix

The replaced slide, verbatim. Bullets on the left: the ultimate goal in GT is usually to identify an equilibrium point, where each player's strategy is their best response to the strategies of the other players. There doesn't have to be only one! And an equilibrium point won't always be the most optimal solution! But at its core, game theory explores and finds strategies that generate equilibria. On the right, a payoff matrix: you on the rows, others on the columns, each choosing strategy A or B. Both A: 3 each. You A, they B: 1 and 4. You B, they A: 4 and 1. Both B: 2 each. B is circled as each player's dominant strategy and a red box marks both-B as the equilibrium.
