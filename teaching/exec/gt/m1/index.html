MODULE 1

The first rule of game theory: it’s not (just) about you!

  • Whatever action one entity does must affect the other side, and vice versa
  • Everything you do has consequences for both you and your opponent
  • Equally, everything your opponent does has consequences for you and your opponent
  • AND…you both know this to be true!
  • This can be thought of as the requirement for common knowledge
MODULE 1

Common knowledge and equilibrium

  • The ultimate goal in GT is to identify an equilibrium point – where each player’s strategy is their best response to the strategies of the other players
  • There doesn’t have to be only one!
  • And an equilibrium point won’t always be the most optimal solution!
  • But at its core, game theory explores and finds strategies that generate equilibria to explain real-world behaviors

A typical example of using common knowledge to find equilibrium

  • I know to stop on red and go on green
  • You know that I know to stop on red and go on green
  • When the light turns green for you, you proceed safely using common knowledge
  • And, therefore, we get a nice equilibrium of stop/go traffic…

MODULE 1 · ADDED VALUE

Added value game

  • Game theory can result in paradoxical or unusual strategic choices if players maximize self-interest
  • Let’s consider the idea of added value
  • I have one white card for every person in this training – and each of you has one yellow card
  • RRPF management has agreed to pay anyone who turns in a pair of white and yellow cards £100
  • You can make deals with me individually, but you cannot band together (this is key!)
  • Make me an offer to purchase my card

MODULE 1 · ADDED VALUE

Added value matters

  • Because three of you are guaranteed to end up with nothing, I can walk away from any individual interaction without losing anything – you’re lucky if I give you £10!
  • The key stipulation was that you could not band together – what would the difference have been if you could have collectively interacted with me?
MODULE 1 · ADDED VALUE

Holland Sweetener gave its added value away

  • Holland Sweetener built a $50 million plant to take on NutraSweet, the sweetener in Diet Coke and Diet Pepsi, when its patent ran out
  • Coke and Pepsi used Holland as a bargaining chip to pay NutraSweet $200 million a year less, and Holland got nothing
  • Later, Holland pointed out that without it Coke and Pepsi would depend on NutraSweet alone again, and it won a guaranteed contract from one of them in return for staying
  • If your being in the game gives the buyer leverage, get paid to play before you give it away
