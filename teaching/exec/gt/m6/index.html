MODULE 6 · THE PENALTY KICK

So how should a penalty kicker behave?

  • In this case, there is no (pure strategy) Nash equilibrium
  • If the kicker always chooses left, the goalie can choose left and drop the kicker’s percentage to 58
  • If the kicker always chooses right, the goalie can choose right and drop the kicker’s percentage to 70
  • Of the two, the kicker would prefer right-right to left-left, but can the kicker do better?
  • Yes – by employing a mixed strategy and making it so that it doesn’t matter which direction the goalie guesses!

MODULE 6 · PETE CARROLL

Pete Carroll, game theorist?

  • Super Bowl XLIX, February 2015. Seattle trail by four with 26 seconds left, second down on New England’s one-yard line
  • Marshawn Lynch, the best short-yardage runner in football, is standing in the backfield. Everyone in the stadium expects the run
  • Seattle throw a quick slant. Malcolm Butler intercepts it, and the game is over
  • It was called the worst play call in Super Bowl history. Was it?
MODULE 6 · PETE CARROLL

The worst call, or the worst luck?

  • If Seattle run every time from the one, the defense only has to stop the run
  • A team that sometimes passes is harder to defend on every play – including the runs
  • The call was part of a mix. The interception was the worst luck
  • So: do you judge your people on their decisions, or on their outcomes?
MODULE 6 · IN BUSINESS

Mixed strategies in business

  • The most obvious application of mixed strategies to the business world comes from organizational efforts to motivate compliance
  • Organizations and institutions want people to behave, but can’t always monitor them
  • So, they introduce selective enforcement, a mixed strategy of enforcement somewhere between 0% and 100%
  • Think about parking meters, speed limits and stop lights: the police combine “random” enforcement with excessive penalties to deter people without actually monitoring them
MODULE 6 · IN BUSINESS

Where else mixing pays

Anywhere someone would exploit you if they could predict you

MODULE 6 · THE INSPECTION GAME

So how should a regulator behave?

  • Constantly inspecting every tiny step is impractical at scale
  • Always trusting the paperwork incentivises the shop to skip steps, so the shop needs to be at least occasionally inspected to keep it honest
  • The game has no pure strategy equilibrium
  • The ideal strategy is to inspect at least 20% of the time, which keeps compliance at 90% or more
  • The regulator’s job is not to be everywhere. It is to be unpredictable

MODULE 6 · ROCK, PAPER, SCISSORS

Rock, paper, scissors and strategy

  • The best strategy is to mix with 1/3 probability of each option, for an average payoff of 0
  • Since the game is symmetrical, both sides have identical strategies (1/3, 1/3, 1/3) which is the Nash equilibrium
  • And yet – nobody is really random!
  • We fall into subconscious patterns even if we don’t realize it, which makes the (1/3, 1/3, 1/3) strategy impossible
