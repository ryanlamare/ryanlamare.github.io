Southwest Airlines

Outtake Stood as slide 13 of 28 · cut 16 Sep 2026 · it and Big Tech and AI made one point (everyone pays, so the advantage is gone), and Southwest ended open seating in January 2026; the dates are corrected here so it can go straight back
  • For fifty years Southwest did not allocate seats – boarding position decided seating (assigned seats arrived only in January 2026)
  • EarlyBird check-in: $10 to be checked in automatically, 24 hours ahead
  • Paying is individually rational, so almost everyone pays
  • If everyone pays, the advantage is gone – Southwest can no longer guarantee an A group seat
  • $98m of revenue in year one, later ~$400m a year – and customers sued

MODULE 4 · THE RULES

Six weeks at the junction

Outtake Stood as slide 5 of 27 · reworded 20 Sep 2026 · the rules of the networked game: hidden rivals, a price held on any phone
  • Every Monday you post your price without seeing theirs. Then both signs go up, and everybody sees everything.
  • Weeks 3 and 6 pay double. Before each one you may ask for a two-minute meeting, one person from each station. It happens only if both stations ask, and nothing said in it binds anyone.
  • You will not be told which station you are playing. Only a meeting will tell you.
  • Hold a price on any of your phones. That is your price for the week.

MODULE 4 · OVERCOMING

Four lessons

Axelrod  ·  what to take away
  1. 1Don’t be envious. Your own total is the key, not whether you beat the other side.
  2. 2Don’t be the first to defect. That decision is rarely recoverable.
  3. 3Reciprocate both ways. Answer restraint with restraint, defection with defection.
  4. 4Don’t be too clever. You can’t cooperate with a strategy you can’t work out.

Big Tech and AI

Outtake Stood as slide 14 of 29 · cut 23 Sep 2026 (Ryan: replaced by Why are AI labs asking to be regulated?; the spending figure had dated)
  • Big Tech is building AI capacity faster than it can prove demand for it
  • Spending will not peak until 2028, at around $1 trillion
  • Davidson Kempner’s CIO: “You have to invest in it because your peers are investing in it”
  • If the other labs don’t build the more powerful model, you should, to take the market. If they do, you must, to keep it. So every lab races, and where does it end?
  • A slower build matched to real demand would leave every firm better off
  • Think of the 2023 open letter asking every lab to “pause” giant AI training for six months: thousands signed, and nobody paused
