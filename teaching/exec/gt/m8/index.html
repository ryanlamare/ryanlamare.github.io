Why do people lie? Because sometimes telling the truth is against their own strategic interests
A self-interested claim
“If the Archbishop of Canterbury says he believes in God, that’s all in the way of business”
A costly admission
“but if he says he doesn’t, one can take it he means what he says”

MODULE 8

Manipulating information

You know something the other side does not
Conceal information, or reveal misleading information
Reveal selected information truthfully
You want to find out what the other side knows
Elicit information, or filter truth from falsehood
Deliberately remain ignorant
But how can you do any of these if you and the other side both know that talk is cheap?
MODULE 8

Signaling, signal jamming, and screening

Three devices can be used to manipulate information:
Signaling
Favorable leakage of information, or actions taken to have the other side believe your information is good
Signal jamming
Acting to reduce or eliminate the leakage of unfavorable information
Screening
Forcing the other side to reveal that side’s true information

A car market game

  • Half of you have been allocated as car buyers and half are car sellers
  • There are two types of cars available in this market: lemons (bad quality cars) and peaches (good quality cars)

  • Also keep in mind that sellers want to get as much as possible irrespective of whether the car is a lemon or a peach
  • Sellers should show buyers what’s written on their phones when buyers approach them

MODULE 8

Adverse selection

People know their own propensity for risk far better than insurance companies do
One premium for everyone, set at the average risk
→
The safe drivers find it too dear and leave. Only the bad risks stay
People select themselves into groups based on their private information, and by doing so reveal it. So companies offer several choices as a screening mechanism: a risky driver, offered low premiums for low coverage or high premiums for high coverage, selects into the second
MODULE 8

Information asymmetries at the rental-car desk

  • Jerry thinks a reservation gets him a car. The company knows a reservation only holds the possibility of a car
  • The clerk goes to check with the manager, and Jerry can see them talking through the window but can’t hear a word
  • Jerry takes the insurance, because he’s going to beat the hell out of the car: he opts in knowing his own private information
  • Had he turned it down, he would have revealed that he planned to drive carefully
Seinfeld, the rental-car desk

Credible signals

The warranty

The marble countertop

MODULE 8

The dog that didn’t bark

Signaling can get messy. Sometimes one side will expect a signal, and the other will not provide it
“The curious incident of the dog in the night-time”: signaling by not signaling
When Netflix said it would stop reporting its subscriber numbers, its shares fell 9% the next day: what does a company signal by not signaling?
“I’m not political. I don’t vote.”  “You are political, because you don’t vote.”
Keep this in mind when you’re hiring job candidates, too!

Pooling and separating equilibria

Cleanliness is a potentially useful signal of the quality of a house. But all sellers know they need to clean their houses, so even those with terrible underlying problems are clean
Pooling
All different types, good and bad, end up taking the same action, rendering it useless
Separating
The good types signal their goodness to me
How might I find a separating equilibrium when I’m in the market for a house?
