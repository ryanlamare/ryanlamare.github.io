MODULE 2 · ROLLBACK

Characteristics of game trees

1
Game trees consist of nodes, branches, and paths of play
2
Payoffs sit at the terminal nodes
3
Trees can be “pruned” as decisions are made
4
“Rollback”: start at the end (what is my goal?) and reason backward
5
Rollback equilibrium: every player choosing their optimal strategy

MODULE 2 · TWO FIRMS

Boeing versus Airbus

  • Airbus enters into the market for planes, and Boeing has a sequential choice: accommodate or block entry
  • Boeing blocks entry with a price war, and the lower prices win large orders from Singapore Airlines, Delta, etc.
  • But the cost cuts came through workforce reductions, orders were delayed, and Airbus sold more planes than Boeing
In October 1997 Boeing halted 747 production for 20 days; the production crisis cost it $2.6 billion and its first annual loss since 1959

A game of backward reasoning

  • Twenty-three cards are on your phone, and will be selected by you and your neighbor sequentially
  • You take turns removing 1, 2, or 3 cards
  • Whoever takes the last card wins
  • Play it on your phones: whoever’s first name is nearest to A makes the first move

The ultimatum game

Proposer’s shareResponder’s share
£500£500
  • £1,000 is available to be split between you and the person next to you.
  • After any discussion you want, the person whose first name is nearest to A proposes a split from their phone
  • Once the proposal is locked in, the responder can only accept or reject
  • If the responder rejects the proposal, nobody gets anything

MODULE 2 · THE CENTIPEDE GAME

The centipede game

  • £100 is on the table. Player 1 can take it, or pass
  • Each pass adds another £100, and the choice switches sides
  • At £1,000 the game ends, and whoever’s turn it is takes it all
  • Play it in pairs, on your phones; this time whoever’s first name is nearest to Z is Player 1

MODULE 2

The limits of sequential reasoning

1
Complexity of drawing game trees to completion (even tic-tac-toe is hard!)
2
Requirement of complete information (in reality, uncertainty plagues us all)
3
Limits of rationality in sequential decision-making (think of the games we just played)
