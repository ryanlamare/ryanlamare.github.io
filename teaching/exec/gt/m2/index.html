MODULE 2 · ROLLBACK

Characteristics of game trees

1
Game trees consist of nodes, branches, and paths of play
2
Payoffs sit at the terminal nodes
3
Trees can be “pruned” as decisions are made
4
“Rollback”: start at the end (what is my goal?) and reason backward
5
Rollback equilibrium: every player choosing their optimal strategy

MODULE 2 · THE CENTIPEDE GAME

The centipede game

  • £100 is on the table. Player 1 can take it, or pass
  • Each pass adds another £100, and the choice switches sides
  • At £1,000 the game ends, and whoever’s turn it is takes it all
  • Play it in pairs, on your phones; this time whoever’s first name is nearest to Z is Player 1

The limits of sequential reasoning

1
Complexity of drawing game trees to completion (even tic-tac-toe is hard!)
2
Requirement of complete information (in reality, uncertainty plagues us all)
3
Limits of rationality in sequential decision-making (think of the games we just played)
MODULE 2 · WHAT DID WE LEARN

What did we learn?

Think forward, reason backward
Start at the end, and prune your way back to today
Who makes the second move?
Future you is not today’s you, and the other side is not you either
First mover isn’t always ahead
Moving first frames the game; it doesn’t win it
You don’t need the whole tree
Nobody charts every path; find who moves last
Real people don’t roll back
A lowball costs the deal, a grab costs the relationship
