FromHead of ProductionToLine producer, Blue SkiesReThe Meridian

You are the line producer on Blue Skies, an eight-part drama for a streaming service about a small British airline in 1964. The director has one line she will not cross: the aircraft has to be real. After two years of looking, the team has found the only restored Hawkridge Meridian left in Europe, a 1962 four-engine airliner. The late owner’s executor, Helen Marsh, will open sealed offers at 5 pm on Friday and sell to the best one. There is no second round, and she wants the aircraft collected within 14 days.

The guide price is £400,000 to £600,000. Finance has authorised you to offer up to £900,000 and not a penny more. The streaming service will not reopen the budget, and every pound spent on the aircraft is a pound the director cannot spend on the night shoots she has been fighting for.

Filming starts on 16 March at a disused airfield in Lincolnshire, which the production has leased until 31 August for sets and storage. The Meridian’s scenes are scheduled for weeks three to ten, after which the unit moves to studio stages in London for the interiors. The art department will repaint the aircraft in the colours of the series’ fictional airline, Albion Air; putting the original colours back would cost about £45,000.

There is one piece of small print you cannot argue with. The completion guarantor, the insurer that guarantees the series gets finished, requires that anything the production buys for more than £100,000 is sold within 60 days of the last day of filming. Nobody on your team knows who buys a 1960s airliner.

You have heard that one other buyer is circling: the Northfield Air Heritage Museum. Museums have not been kind to Harrow. Two years ago one refused you a loan and told a newspaper that your productions “treat heritage as scenery”. Assume Northfield wants the Meridian for permanent display, and will fight for it. If the museum bids too, you may have to offer well above the guide price to be sure of winning, and museums can find money when they want something badly.

Marketing, meanwhile, is still hunting for an unusual venue for the series launch in November.

Before we make an offer, I want you to talk to Northfield and persuade the museum not to stand in our way. If it helps, you may offer the museum something for standing aside, as long as the total stays within the budget. Its director has agreed to see you before Friday.

Your objective: get Blue Skies its aircraft, at the lowest possible cost to the production.
