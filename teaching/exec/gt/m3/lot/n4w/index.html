Your brief · keep it to yourselfLarkfield Pictures
FromManaging DirectorToProducer, Jet SetReThe Meridian

You are the producer of Jet Set, a feature film for a broadcaster about the first years of the jet age. The director will not compromise: the aircraft has to be real. Only one restored Hawkridge Meridian, the 1962 four-engine airliner the story needs, is left in Europe. The broadcaster will fund the film only if the aircraft is secured this week; without it, Jet Set is cancelled, and the studio may not survive the loss.

Principal photography begins in July with the interiors, on stages in Bristol. The Meridian’s scenes follow at a disused airfield in Kent, for eight weeks from 1 September, and the unit finishes on location in Scotland before Christmas. The schedule is confidential: a rival that knows your dates can race you to the screen.

The late owner’s executor, Helen Marsh, will sell the Meridian to the highest bidder by 5 pm on Friday. Her agent expects offers of around £450,000. The studio has authorised you to bid as high as £750,000, but every pound above the guide comes out of an effects budget that is already cut to the bone.

You have learned that Harrow Pictures is also urgently trying to buy the Meridian, for a rival series set in the same years; studios make twin films all the time. There is no love lost between you. Larkfield hired Harrow’s showrunner last year, Harrow sued, and the case is still in court, and each studio suspects the other of leaking its scripts. Harrow also has form: it once bought the film rights to a bestseller and shelved it, just so that a rival could not adapt it. If Harrow gets the Meridian, assume you will never see it again.

Before making your offer, you have decided to talk to Harrow’s line producer, to persuade them not to stand in your way. Prepare for your meeting.

Your objective: get Jet Set its aircraft, at the lowest possible cost to the production.
When time is up, Harrow’s producer types in what your pair agreed.
