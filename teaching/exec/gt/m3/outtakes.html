Slide 3 of 4: A zero-sum game in your own market

Stood as slide 12 of 22. Lead line: a mature market is a fixed pie — every point of share you win comes straight off your rival's total. A three-by-three payoff matrix of your share of the market. Both you (rows) and your closest rival (columns) choose between promote hard, match the market, and pull back spend. Values by row: 50, 60, 70; 40, 50, 60; 30, 40, 50. Steps eliminate one line at a time: your rival will never pull back spend, so that column disappears; with it gone, pulling back can never be your best move either, so that row disappears; your rival is better off promoting hard than matching, so the matching column disappears; that leaves your choice — 50 beats 40 — so the matching row disappears; and the red box marks the equilibrium where both sides promote hard, a 50/50 split.

Slide 4 of 4: The limits of zero-sum thinking, as it stood

Stood as slide 21 of 22; only its last chip was cut. Lead line: zero-sum games require that, in order for one side to win, the other must lose. The two pie pictograms: zero-sum, today's games, a fixed pie; and non-zero-sum, highlighted. Reveals: most of your decisions are not restricted to win/lose, which is where we go next.
