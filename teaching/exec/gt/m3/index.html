MODULE 3

Introduction to simultaneous zero-sum strategies

Often a player must make a choice without knowing what their rivals have chosen, and vice versa
Two types of simultaneous games:
Zero (Constant) sum
Win/lose – a fixed pie
Non-zero (variable) sum
Win/win or lose/lose – pie not set
Recall one of the limitations of sequential games: the notion of complete information
Simultaneous games are our first application of incomplete information
Applications: voting behavior; football matches; wages; etc.

MODULE 3 · THE MATRIX

Guidelines for solving zero-sum games

Identify dominant (or superior) strategies
A dominant strategy exists if a player’s payoff from choosing this strategy is always greater than, or at least equal to, the payoff from choosing any other available strategy. If a dominant strategy exists, choose it!
Eliminate dominated (or inferior) strategies
An inferior strategy exists if a player’s payoff from choosing this strategy is always less than the payoff from choosing any other available strategy. If an inferior strategy exists, never choose it!

MODULE 3 · GAME THEORY IN YOUR WORLD

Bidding for the airline’s engine business

  • An airline needs several engines financed and supported over the coming years, and it will spread its business across two rival bidders aiming for maximum market share
  • You and a rival bidder are each pitching for as much of that business as you can each get
  • Each of you simultaneously chooses what to emphasize in your pitch to the airline:
  • Cut the price – offer the airline the same terms as your rival, but for less money
  • Promise better terms – keep your price higher, make the terms as attractive as possible to the airline
  • Solve a longstanding concern – pledge to fix a problem the airline has lived with for years
  • Charm the board – focus the pitch not on substance but entirely on goodwill, hospitality, and warm words

a few key takeaways...

zero-sum = fixed pie
One side’s win must be the other side’s loss
Simultaneous
Both move without knowing what the other has chosen
Never play an inferior strategy
If a move is worse whatever they do, cross it out
SEEK OUT dominant strategIES
