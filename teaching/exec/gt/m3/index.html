MODULE 3

Introduction to simultaneous zero-sum strategies

Often a player must make a choice without knowing what their rivals have chosen, and vice versa
Two types of simultaneous games:
Zero (Constant) sum
Win/lose – a fixed pie
Non-zero (variable) sum
Win/win or lose/lose – pie not set
Recall one of the limitations of sequential games: the notion of perfect information
Simultaneous games are our first application of imperfect information
Applications: voting behavior; football matches; wages; etc.

MODULE 3

Zero-sum examples: Jeopardy champion Arthur Chu

  • Only the winner keeps their money and comes back the next day, so every Jeopardy game has one winner and two losers
  • Arthur Chu started at the bottom of the board, where the biggest clues and almost nine in ten Daily Doubles are, and jumped between categories to hunt them
  • There are only three Daily Doubles in a game, so when he found one in a category he knew nothing about, he bet just $5 to keep it from the others
  • IBM’s Watson computer was built to play the same way when it beat Ken Jennings in 2011: find the Daily Doubles, and deny them to everyone else

MODULE 3 · THE MATRIX

Guidelines for solving zero-sum games

Identify dominant (or superior) strategies
A dominant strategy exists if a player’s payoff from choosing this strategy is always greater than, or at least equal to, the payoff from choosing any other available strategy. If a dominant strategy exists, choose it!
Eliminate dominated (or inferior) strategies
An inferior strategy exists if a player’s payoff from choosing this strategy is always less than the payoff from choosing another available strategy. If an inferior strategy exists, never choose it!
Once it’s ruled out, look again at the smaller matrix that’s left, for both players.

Bidding for the airline’s engine business

You and a rival bidder are each pitching for as much of that business as you can each get

Each of you simultaneously chooses what to emphasize in your pitch to the airline:

Cut the price
Offer the airline the same terms as your rival, but for less money
Promise better terms
Keep your price higher, make the terms as attractive as possible to the airline
Solve a longstanding concern
Pledge to fix a problem the airline has lived with for years
Charm the board
Focus the pitch not on substance but entirely on goodwill, hospitality, and warm words

MODULE 3 · YOUR INDUSTRY

Zero-sum games in aviation

A fixed pie
Heathrow slots
  • In 2016, Oman Air paid Air France-KLM $75 million for one pair of slots
  • “Use it or lose it”: in winter 2021–22 Lufthansa said it would have to fly about 18,000 unnecessary flights to keep its slots from rivals
Hotelling on a clock
Why do all the flights leave at 8 am?
  • On routes with more competition, rival airlines bunch their departures closer together, like vendors moving to the middle of the beach
  • Where scheduling rules loosened, they spread out a little, to avoid competing head-on on price
