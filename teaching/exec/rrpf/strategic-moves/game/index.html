THE STANDOFF
One contract · four rounds · three cards

You and one other team are negotiating a single renewal. There is a fixed amount of value in the contract, and the only question is how it gets divided between you. Neither side can walk away and find someone else: it is this contract with this counterparty, or nothing.

You will have four attempts to agree. Every attempt that fails costs both of you, because the value in the contract falls while you are still arguing about it. If you have not agreed by the end of the fourth round, you both walk away with nothing at all.

How a deal happens

  • Each round, both sides privately write down a demand, meaning the number of points you are claiming from the contract. Demands are revealed together.
  • If the two demands add up to no more than the value on the table, you have a deal. Each side banks exactly the number it asked for, the negotiation is over, and nothing further is played.
  • If the two demands add up to more than the value on the table, there is no deal this round. The value drops and you go again.
  • Anything left over when a deal is struck is lost. It does not get shared out. Asking for too little costs you just as surely as asking for too much, only more quietly.

Note what this does to a demand you were comfortable with in round one. Nothing about your position has changed, but the room it needs to fit into has.

Your three cards

You hold three cards, one of each kind below. Each may be played once, and you may play at most one card per round. A card is played out loud, before demands are written, so the other side hears it and so does the room. Once played, a card cannot be taken back, and the facilitator enforces it against you whether or not it is still working in your favour.

Two of the three act on the round that follows, so The Walk and The Concession cannot be played in round four. The Floor can be played in any round, including the last.

1

The Floor

Unconditional · a commitment
“My demand will never go below X.”

Name a number out loud. From this round until the end of the game, none of your demands may fall below it. You have given up the ability to concede past that point, and the other side knows it.

That is the whole idea. If they believe you, their best remaining move is to fit themselves around you, and you take the larger share without having to argue for it.

It binds you. The floor cannot be withdrawn, reduced, or forgotten, and it stays in force even if it turns out to make agreement impossible.
2

The Walk

Conditional · a threat
“If your demand this round is above Y, my demand next round will be 100.”

Name the number Y out loud. If their demand this round comes in above it, you are required to demand the entire contract next round, which guarantees that round fails and shrinks the value again for both of you.

If their demand comes in at Y or below, nothing happens and the card is simply spent.

It binds you. If the condition is triggered you must carry it out, including in the final round, where carrying it out means you both score zero.
3

The Concession

Conditional · a promise
“If your demand this round is Y or below, my next demand will be at least Z lower than this one.”

Name Y and Z out loud. If they meet the condition, you are required to move by at least Z in the following round. If they do not, nothing happens and the card is spent.

This is the card that buys movement from the other side rather than forcing it, and it is the only one of the three that makes agreement easier rather than harder.

It binds you. If they comply, you concede, regardless of how the round turned out or how you feel about it afterwards.

A worked round

For clarity, not as advice

Round 1. 100 on the table. You play The Floor at 55. Both sides write down demands: you 60, them 55. That sums to 115, so no deal, and the value falls to 90.

Round 2. 90 on the table. You are now bound at 55 or above. You demand 55, they demand 35. That sums to 90, so deal. You bank 55 and they bank 35.

Had they held at 45 instead, you would have summed to 100 against a table of 90, failed again, and faced round three with 80 available and your own floor still sitting at 55.

Your objective
Bank as many points as you can. A deal you resent still counts. No deal counts for nothing.

Rules

  • Two minutes with your team before each round.
  • Cards are played out loud before demands are written, and are recorded at the front of the room. Anything you have not said out loud does not bind you and does not count.
  • Demands are private until both are revealed together.
  • No other contact with the other side. Cards are the only way to communicate, which is the point: what you can make them believe is limited to what you can make binding.
  • Deals end the game. Once the demands fit, you are done, and any cards still in your hand are worth nothing.
