PRICE WARS
Alba Fuels vs Batia Service Station · six trading weeks

You run one of two petrol stations on opposite corners of the same junction, on a busy A road with nothing else for four miles in either direction. About four thousand cars pass every day and every one of them can read both of your signs before deciding which side to pull into.

One of you is Alba Fuels, thirty years on this corner, lower overheads, an older set of regulars and a manager who has never once been undercut without answering it. The other is Batia Service Station, newer, six pumps to Alba’s four, a car wash that brings people in, and a bank loan that needs servicing. I will tell you which one you are.

Beyond that you sell identical fuel. Same supplier, same specification, same card readers, same overpriced sandwiches. The number on the sign is the only real difference between you.

Every Monday morning you post your price for the week. You post it without seeing theirs. Once both signs are up, they stay up until the following Monday, and everybody can see everything. You will be doing this for six weeks, and neither of you is going anywhere.

Your two choices

Option one

Post £1.50

Your standard price. You earn 30p a litre. If they post the same, the traffic splits between you roughly as it always does and you both have a good week.

Option two

Post £1.40

You earn 20p a litre instead of 30p, but if their sign still says £1.50 then almost every car on that road turns into your forecourt instead of theirs.

Reading the payoffs

  • Both post £1.50 (12, 12). Around 40,000 litres each at 30p, so £12,000 apiece. Nobody wins a driver and nobody loses one.
  • You post £1.40 and they hold. Your margin drops to 20p, but volume more than doubles to about 90,000 litres, so you take £18,000. They keep the handful of regulars who never look at the sign: £2,000.
  • Both post £1.40 (9, 9). Cheap fuel does pull a few extra cars onto the road, so about 45,000 litres each, but only at 20p. £9,000 each. You have handed £3,000 to the motorists of the district and gained nothing from each other.

Week 4: the one conversation

Before week four, and only before week four, you may send one person to meet one person from the other station for two minutes. The meeting happens only if both stations ask for it. If either declines, there is no meeting.

Nothing agreed in that meeting is written down, and nothing said in it binds you in any way when you set your price.

Rules

  • Six weeks, and one price decision each week.
  • Three minutes with your own team before each week. Decide one week at a time rather than planning all six now.
  • Give your price to the facilitator privately. Both signs then go up together, and the scores go on the board at the front.
  • No contact with the other station at any point, except the two-minute meeting before week four, and only if both stations have asked for it.
  • You are trying to finish with the largest total. Not to beat the other station, and not to be liked by them. The largest total.
Your objective
Finish the six weeks with the largest total on the board.
