Slide 4 of 11: Brief Discussion of Week 4 Content

A coordination game has several equilibria; both sides want to land together. There is no dominant strategy, so we use best response: a Nash outcome is a mutual best response. A focal point is the equilibrium expectations converge on, made obvious by context. Three HR games: stag hunt where interests are aligned, battle of the sexes where they differ, and chicken where they are opposed.

Slide 5 of 11: The Investment Game (play)

The whole class plays at once. Each of you can put ten dollars into a shared venture, or not invest and keep it. It pays off only if nearly everyone invests: then your ten becomes fifteen. Invest while others do not and you lose your ten.

Slide 6 of 11: The Investment Game (debrief)

What you just played, as a payoff matrix: a stag hunt. Mutual Invest pays plus five each; nobody investing pays zero each; both are equilibria. Invest while everyone else does not and you lose ten, the worst case. Not investing while everyone else invests and you get zero while they gain. Two equilibria, and trust decides which one the room lands on. The matrix is the lesson, not part of the game.

Slide 7 of 11: When Does the Team Meet? (play)

A battle of the sexes. You have merged into one team and need one weekly meeting time. Team Sales wants the 9 AM slot, since they are out with clients all afternoon; Team Engineering wants 2 PM, since mornings are for deep work. Pick the same slot or half the team is always missing, but each side prefers its time.

Slide 8 of 11: When Does the Team Meet? (debrief)

What you just played, as a payoff matrix. Both want the meeting to happen, but on a different slot. If both pick 9 AM it is three to two; both pick 2 PM is two to three; a split fails at zero and zero. Two equilibria, and the fight is over whose slot. The matrix is the discussion, not part of the game.

Slide 9 of 11: The Focal-Point Quiz

Answer each question to match your classmates; you score only if about ninety percent agree. No conferring: you are coordinating on instinct! We reveal the answers together afterward and see where the class converged. The point: focal points form from shared context and common knowledge. An interactive version of the quiz is linked from this slide.
