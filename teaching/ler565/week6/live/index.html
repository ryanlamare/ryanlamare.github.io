Slide 4 of 8: Brief Discussion of Week 6 Content

A strategic move is a commitment, threat, or promise that changes the game in your favor. It lives on credibility: it only works if others believe you will follow through. The strongest moves are observable and irreversible, so you take your own options away on purpose. First-mover advantage, reputation, and burning bridges are all strategic moves.

Slide 5 of 8: The Azee Tournament

All games are played at azee dot mattle dot online; log in and grab your username. Enter your name and username in the Google Sheet, which is how we track the bracket. I will randomly assign groups and send you to breakout rooms; one person creates the game on a normal board at fast speed, five minutes. Record the winner in the sheet and come back to the main room. If you get knocked out, play the other eliminated players or observe; do not miss the final.

Slide 6 of 8: The Bracket

The path to champion: groups of two to three to start, then one-on-one battles. First round, then the Sweet 16, quarter-finals, semi-finals, and the final. No boss battle this time; the tournament is the finale. A link opens the live bracket board.

Slide 7 of 8: Due This Week

Three things, all due Sunday August 9: the Week 6 quiz, fifty points; Podcast Analysis Assignment number two, one hundred twenty-five points; and Azee Reflection number three, on information asymmetries or strategic moves, eighty-three point three points. Find them all in Canvas.

Slide 8 of 8: That's the Course

Six weeks of games: prisoner's dilemmas, sequential games, coordination, information asymmetries, and strategic moves. You played every one, and saw where each shows up in real HR. Put it together and you can read, and shape, the games at work. Thanks for playing.
