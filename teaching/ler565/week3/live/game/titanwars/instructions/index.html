LER 565  ·  HRM & STRATEGY  ·  WEEK 3
Titan Wars
at Zephyr Technologies

You are an Engineering Manager at Zephyr Technologies, a consumer-electronics company about to launch a product it expects to disrupt the industry. The visionary CEO has announced Project Titan, a top-secret initiative to build a revolutionary handheld device. Instead of handing it to one department, the CEO has launched a rigorous internal "bake-off", pitting two parallel engineering groups against each other to design the operating system. Both teams report directly to the CEO, who each round allocates budgets by evaluating the teams on how effectively they deliver each month.

Two rival teams bet on how to build Titan: same device, but opposite approaches.

Team Alpha · Shrink it down

The desktop, slimmed

Take the company’s powerful, full-featured desktop OS and strip it down to fit a tiny handheld. Proven and capable, but heavy and hard to slim.

Team Beta · Build it up

The gadget, grown

Take the simple, lightweight OS from a small accessory device and build it up into a full handheld platform. Nimble and efficient, but a lot still to add.

Your Strategic Choices

Each round, your team must secretly choose one of two work standards for your engineers.

Keep it Regular

Normal Hours

A strict 40-hour week. You protect your engineers from burnout and keep a steady, high-quality, sustainable output.

push for Crunch

Grind Mode

80-hour weeks and all-nighters. You push the team into overdrive to inflate your feature delivery for the round.

Reading the Payoffs

  • Both Normal Hours (+$3M, +$3M). Both teams hit targets sustainably without burning out. The CEO is satisfied with both products and raises both teams’ rewards.
  • One Crunches, one doesn’t. If Alpha crunches and Beta holds Normal: (+$6M, −$2M). If Beta crunches and Alpha holds: (−$2M, +$6M). The crunching team delivers ~20% more features; the CEO calls them “geniuses” and hands them the round’s entire budget increase, while the other looks slow and takes a cut.
  • Both Crunch ($0, $0). Neither gains a relative edge, but everyone works extra. The CEO treats 80-hour weeks as the “new normal,” and tired engineers make small mistakes, so budgets hold flat.

Milestone Sprints: Rounds 3 & 5

  • Rounds 3 and 5 are major Prototype Reviews open to the Board. Due to heavy scrutiny, the stakes are higher: all budget impacts are DOUBLED for these rounds.
  • Just before each, you may meet the other side for two minutes. The meeting happens only if both sides elect to meet; if either declines, there is no meeting.
  • If both meet, one representative from each side meets in a private breakout room. Nothing discussed is binding. It is talk, not a contract.

Rules of the Competition

  • The competition lasts exactly six rounds (six product sprint cycles).
  • Internal alignment. Your team is in a private breakout room with three minutes each round to debate strategy.
  • Blind decisions. When I enter your room, tell me your choice (Normal or Crunch). You learn the other team’s choice only after you’ve submitted; both teams see the result before the next round.
  • Strict silos. No communication with the opposing team, except the milestone meetings before Rounds 3 and 5.
  • Use the Team Ledger. Once I have both choices, I announce the result; you record the outcome and track your cumulative budget.
Your sole objective
Maximize your own team’s cumulative budget over the six rounds!
